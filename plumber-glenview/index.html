Repair or replace: the Glenview decision in plain terms

Most Glenview calls come down to one question: patch what is failing, or replace the whole run. A repair makes sense when one fitting, valve or short section has failed and the rest of the pipe is sound. Replacement makes sense when the same line keeps failing or the material itself is the concern.

Because we quote a clear price before work starts, you can compare both paths on paper before anything is opened up. For a buried line, our page on water service line repair has the longer version.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak in Glenview

Water spreads fast across a finished basement or a slab, so the first few minutes matter more than the repair method. Do these while our emergency dispatch line is sending someone out.

  1. Shut off the main water valve, usually near the meter where the line enters the house.
  2. Turn off power to any wet area at the breaker panel if you can reach it without standing in water.
  3. Open a low faucet to drain what is left in the pipes.
  4. Move people, pets and valuables away from the wet area.
  5. If the leak is outside, note where the water is surfacing near the curb or lawn.
  6. Call our 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher what you shut off.

Water service line: repair the section or replace the run

Repair a section

  • Makes sense when the pipe is otherwise sound and a single joint or valve failed.
  • Usually a smaller excavation and a shorter job.
  • Lower cost up front, but another failure on the same old line is possible.

Replace the run

  • Makes sense for repeated leaks, low pressure from corrosion or a line of unknown material.
  • Larger excavation, more restoration, longer project.
  • Higher cost now, usually fewer repeat visits later.

Glenview note: who owns which part of the water line

Glenview is a village in Cook County. Its own Water and Sewer page says it buys most of its water from the Village of Wilmette, which draws from Lake Michigan through two intake pipes. That differs from Park Ridge, so do not assume your water works the same way as a neighbor across the line.

The Village's Lead Services page splits the service line at the buffalo box, with the water main to the box on the public side and the box to the house on the private side. The same page says Glenview banned lead service pipes in 1959, so newer homes in Corporate Glenview are expected to have none, while homes annexed from unincorporated Cook County need a closer look. A leak past the box is your repair.

For permits, the Village's Development Center at Village Hall, 2500 East Lake Avenue, handles building permits and contractor licensing, and the Community Development Department runs that office. Permit rules vary by job, so confirm with the Village before work starts.

Cost-share and what to ask first

The Village approved a lead service line cost-share program in August 2022. As listed on its Lead Services page, it covered the public portion in full, half of the private portion and the new meter, with permit fees waived. Programs change, so ask the Village whether it is still open, and get two written estimates first since the Village asks for them.

Basement water: choosing between a sump pump and an overhead sewer

Glenview's Flood Awareness page lists three sources of basement flooding: overbank river water, sanitary sewer backup and local drainage. It also says several areas are susceptible to sanitary sewer backups into basements, and that many areas built before the early 1970s lack stormwater detention. The Cook County hazard plan records about 200 residents reporting flooding or sewer backup in the April 2013 storms, with over 300 service requests logged.

That matters for your choice. A sump pump handles groundwater at the footing drain. It does not stop sewage coming up through a floor drain, which is what an overhead sanitary sewer conversion is for, and the Village says it has offered one-time cost sharing for residents who convert. If you are unsure which problem you have, sump pump repair is the faster fix and sewer line cleaning is the call for a blocked house sewer.
