A park based area name taken from the city's own sewer plans

Northwest Park Area is not a marketing name. It appears in the Cook County hazard mitigation annex for Park Ridge as an action titled Northwest Park Area sewer replacement, with a listed cost of 16.6 million dollars. It also appears in a September 2017 city sewer study, which describes a sewer separation concept with excavated storage for this part of town.

Park Ridge Park District lists Northwest Park at 1200 N Dee Rd. We use that address as an anchor rather than draw a boundary the city has not published. If you are unsure whether your street is in the area, call and give us the address.

The city's 2017 study also used a storm on April 18, 2013 in this area to test its sewer model. That is a record of a modeling exercise. It does not tell us which houses had problems.

Why a sewer check can matter near planned or active street work

Park Ridge's plans describe separating or enlarging sewers, which means digging in streets. We do not know the schedule for any given block, and you should ask the city. What we can say generally is that when mains change nearby, a camera look at your own lateral gives you a baseline and a record.

Our sewer camera inspection shows the pipe condition from the cleanout to the street connection. If it finds a defect, sewer line repair is the next conversation.

Basement water after rain

A sump pump problem or a drain backup. See sump pump repair.

Sewage smell

A dry trap, a vent problem or a damaged line. A camera check sorts it out.

Verified local facts and what we do not know

Verified: the hazard annex names a Northwest Park Area sewer replacement action. The Park District lists Northwest Park at 1200 N Dee Rd. The city profile lists Dee Road as a Metra station. Citywide, the annex describes an antiquated combination sewer system and records nearly 500 flooded basements on July 23, 2011.

Unknown: the exact boundary, the construction schedule, the age of any particular home's sewer, and which streets flood most often. We do not guess at these.

Before we arrive: sewer backup or a leaking line

If sewage is coming up, keep people and pets out of the area.

  1. Stop using water in the house.
  2. Do not flush toilets or run the washer.
  3. Close the main water valve if a supply line is leaking.
  4. Keep children and pets away from standing water.
  5. Call dispatch and describe where the water appears.
