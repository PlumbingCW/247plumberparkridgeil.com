Water backing up a floor drain in Des Plaines

A gurgling toilet, a washer that spits water into the basement drain, or gray water around the floor drain all say the same thing: the line leaving the house cannot carry what you send it. The plug may sit in your own pipe or in the public sewer in the street.

A simple test sorts it out. If one sink is slow, the clog is near that sink. If the lowest drain in the house backs up while the upstairs fixtures still work, the trouble is on the main line or beyond it, and the longer water stands there, the more floor it reaches.

Common causes

What usually causes a sewer backup here

Roots and grease

Roots slip into joints that have already opened, and kitchen grease cools into a plug.

Combined sewer load

The city's Public Works page counts its sanitary and combined sewers together, 169 miles in all. Where a sewer is combined, a hard rain shares the pipe with household waste.

Flat, slow ground

The city's hazard plan describes the original land as flat, wet prairie that had to be ditched to drain. Water still moves slowly through the low spots.

Sags and breaks

A sunken stretch of pipe holds waste in a pool, and a cracked one lets soil wash in. Only a camera tells you which.

Cable, jet or dig: matching the fix to the pipe

A clog in a sound pipe can be cleared with a cable or a high-pressure water jet. Sewer line cleaning fits that case, and we run a camera afterward so you know the pipe is open and not just briefly clear. If the picture shows a break, a sagging section or roots through a joint, sewer line repair is the lasting answer.

Cleaning a line versus repairing it

Cleaning

  • Quickest way to get drains working again.
  • Right for grease, paper and soft roots.
  • Does not fix a cracked or sunken pipe.

Repair

  • Fixes the break, sag or bad joint itself.
  • Can mean digging or lining, depending on the pipe.
  • Ends repeat backups caused by damage.

Low ground along the river and the creeks

The 2019 Des Plaines annex to the Cook County hazard plan names the Des Plaines River, Weller Creek, Big Bend and the River and Oakton area as places that have flooded. It also lists 245 FEMA repetitive-loss properties, 225 of them residential.

If your home sits near one of those low areas, a working sump pump matters more than it does uphill. Sump pump repair is the usual first call when the pit is full and the pump is silent.

Urgent: act now

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

If water is coming from a pipe, find the main shutoff valve and turn it clockwise until it stops. A break in the street is a call to Public Works, whose main break line is open evenings, weekends and holidays.

If water is coming up a drain instead, stop using every fixture. Each flush or load of laundry adds to what is already rising.

  1. Turn off the main water valve if a pipe is leaking.
  2. Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  3. Stop flushing and stop running the washer or sinks if a drain is backing up.
  4. Move people and pets out of a wet basement.
  5. Lift small valuables off the floor and open a window if there is a sewer smell.
  6. Call our 24/7 line and tell dispatch what you shut off.

Des Plaines note: who owns the pipe and who to call first

Des Plaines is a city in Cook County with a 2020 Census count of 60,675, and the Census Bureau puts owner occupancy at 78.3 percent. The city says it buys drinking water from Chicago and the Northwest Water Commission, while Illinois American Water serves some areas. Your bill shows which applies.

On the sewer side, the city says the private lateral belongs to the property owner, including the part under the street. For a backup, call Public Works first so the city can check the main. If a plumber then finds a blockage in the right-of-way, the city Plumbing Inspector verifies it.
