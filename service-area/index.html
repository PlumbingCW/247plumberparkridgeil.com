Where our plumbers go

Park Ridge (ZIP 60068) is our home base, and calls inside the city are the quickest to reach. From there we cover the towns that share its storms and its aging pipes, listed below with the ZIP codes each one uses.

Distances are approximate straight-line figures from the middle of Park Ridge, and real drive times depend on traffic on Northwest Highway, Touhy Avenue and the Kennedy and Tri-State corridors.

Park Ridge, our home city

Park Ridge has about 39,656 residents and a housing stock that is mostly owner-occupied single-family homes, with townhomes and commercial strips along Northwest Highway and Touhy Avenue. According to the city's water quality report, Park Ridge buys all of its drinking water from Chicago, so the supply comes from Lake Michigan.

If you live here, start with our drain cleaning, sewer line cleaning and sump pump repair pages. Those cover the problems the city's own flood records point to.

Why one line covers several towns

Neighboring towns share the same river, the same storms and many of the same commuter roads, which is why one dispatch line can cover them. Each town page covers its own water utility, permit office and common problems, because those differ from one village to the next.

For example, who owns a sewer lateral and which office issues a permit depends on the municipality. Check the page for your town before you assume the Park Ridge rules apply.

Some addresses near the edges sit in unincorporated Maine Township, and the permit office can differ there. Tell the dispatcher your street address, your ZIP and the name on your water bill, and we will confirm how your property is handled before a plumber is sent.

Neighborhoods just over the line

Several Chicago neighborhoods touch Park Ridge, including Edison Park and Norwood Park, and Harwood Heights sits to the southeast. We take emergency calls from these areas and do not publish separate pages for them, because Chicago plumbing permits and licensing follow different city rules.

If you are in one of these places, call the 24/7 line and the dispatcher will tell you honestly whether we can help and how soon. Local rules differ across the border, so we confirm permit needs for your address first.

How to check coverage fast

Call the 24/7 line and give the dispatcher your street address. If the address falls inside our service area, you will get safety steps and a visit time on the same call. If it falls outside, you will hear that right away, so you can call someone closer without losing time.
