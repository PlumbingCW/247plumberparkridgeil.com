Emergency water heater replacement: when the tank fails tonight

A failed tank can release dozens of gallons onto a basement or garage floor within minutes. Your goal before we arrive is to stop the supply, take away heat or fuel, and protect whatever sits on the floor.

If you smell gas at any point, go outside and call 911 or your gas utility first.

  1. Close the cold water shutoff above the heater, or the main valve if the heater valve will not turn.
  2. Switch off the breaker for an electric unit, only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.
  3. Turn a gas unit's control knob to Off, unless you smell gas, in which case leave first.
  4. Lift stored boxes, tools and electronics off the floor.
  5. Take photographs of the heater, the label and the water for your insurer.
  6. Call our 24/7 line and tell us the fuel type, the tank size on the label and where the heater sits.

How long a water heater should last

The U.S. Department of Energy (energy.gov) lists storage water heaters at about 10 to 15 years, tankless at about 20 years and heat pump units at about 10 to 15 years. Those are typical figures, not a countdown. Water chemistry, flushing habits and the quality of the original installation all shift the real number.

The label on the tank carries a serial number that usually encodes the manufacture date. Read it before you call so we can tell you how old the unit is. If it falls within or past the typical window, the decision above gets easier.

Choose with eyes open

Your options once the old tank goes

Same fuel, same size

The simplest swap. Existing gas line, electric circuit and vent are reused where they pass inspection.

Larger or smaller tank

Right-sizing for the number of people and fixtures in your home can fix a heater that always runs out.

Tankless

Endless hot water at a limited flow rate, but gas line and electric capacity must support it. See tankless water heater for the trade-offs.

Heat pump

Very efficient, but it needs room and surrounding air. Details below.

Gas to electric

Possible when the panel has spare capacity and a circuit can be run. The vent is no longer needed, though a new circuit is.

Electric to gas

Requires a gas line, venting and a combustion air plan. Not every house can host this.

Sizing the new unit

Size is about how much hot water your household draws at the busiest hour, not how big the old tank was. Count the people, bathrooms and big hot water users like a dishwasher or soaking tub. A family that outgrew a small tank will keep running cold until the tank changes.

Oversizing is not free either. A larger tank stores more hot water to keep warm all day and takes more floor and clearance space. We talk through your habits and the closet or basement corner before picking a model.

Heat pump water heaters: efficiency and space

Energy.gov says heat pump water heaters can be two to three times more energy efficient than conventional electric resistance water heaters. They pull heat from the surrounding air, so they also cool the room they sit in.

That matters for placement. The same source says the location should stay between 40 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit year round and have at least 1,000 cubic feet of air space around the unit. A small, cold closet is a poor fit, while a larger basement with some excess heat is a better one.

Venting, relief valve and expansion tank

Section 890.1230 of the Illinois Plumbing Code requires a relief valve on all equipment that heats or stores hot water, with a discharge outlet connected indirectly to waste and metallic discharge piping. We check that on every replacement.

Venting, expansion tank and similar code items vary by fuel, house and what the existing system looks like, so we confirm them on site and before work starts rather than guess from a phone call.

Park Ridge note: permits and inspection

The City of Park Ridge FAQ says Article 15 of the Municipal Code requires a permit for all plumbing installations or alterations, and a certificate of inspection and approval before a new plumbing system is used. A replacement heater is a new installation, so expect the permit and inspection step to be part of the job.

Permit rules vary by job, so we confirm with the Park Ridge Building Department what applies to your house. If you are comparing quotes, ask each one who pulls the permit.
