120V Tankless Water Heater for a Shower in Park Ridge, IL

A 120V tankless water heater generally cannot heat enough water for a full shower. A shower needs a lot of heat per minute, and an ordinary 120V circuit cannot deliver it. Small 120V units are built for hand sinks and similar low-flow taps, not for a shower that runs 2 gallons or more each minute.

If you want instant hot water for a shower in Park Ridge, IL, you are looking at a 240V electric unit or a gas tankless heater, or a regular tank. The rest of this page shows the math, what 120V does well, and how to choose.

The electrical reasoning, in plain numbers

The EPA WaterSense program says standard showerheads use 2.5 gallons per minute (gpm), and WaterSense labeled ones use no more than 2.0 gpm. So the heater has to warm at least 2 gallons every minute while you stand there.

Heating water takes a fixed amount of energy. A gallon weighs about 8.3 pounds, one BTU raises a pound by one degree F, and one kilowatt equals about 3,412 BTU per hour. Take a 2.0 gpm shower with a 55 degree F rise (say 50 degrees in, 105 out). That works out to roughly 16 kilowatts, by standard conversions. Your real incoming temperature will change the number.

At 120 volts, 16 kilowatts would mean about 134 amps. A home branch circuit is 15 or 20 amps. Even a dedicated 20-amp 120V circuit tops out near 2.4 kilowatts, which is about one-seventh of what that shower needs.

What a real 120V unit does

The Stiebel Eltron Mini 2.5-1 is a typical 120V point-of-use tankless heater. Its listing shows 2.4 kW, a dedicated 20-amp circuit, and a flow of about 0.5 gpm with the supplied aerator, and it is sold for single handwashing sinks. Run the same arithmetic, and 2.4 kW at 0.5 gpm gives a rise of about 33 degrees F.

That is fine for a hand sink and nowhere near what a shower needs. Even if you throttled a shower to a trickle, you would stand under 0.5 gpm of warm water. Another common 120V product, the Eemax MiniTank, is a 1.4 kW unit with a 1.5-gallon storage tank, so it is a small tank heater, not an on-demand one, and it also targets sink use.

What a shower really needs: a sizing checklist

  • ✓Find the gpm of your showerhead. Standard heads are 2.5 gpm, and WaterSense heads are 2.0 gpm or less.
  • ✓Add the flow of anything else that might run at once, such as a dishwasher or a second shower.
  • ✓Decide the temperature you want at the head, then subtract the coldest incoming water you see in January.
  • ✓Ask the manufacturer's sizing chart what flow the unit delivers at that temperature rise.
  • ✓Check the electrical service. A 240V electric tankless may need more panel capacity than the house has.
  • ✓For gas, confirm the gas line size, venting route and condensate drain.
  • ✓Weigh the simple case too: a tank heater has no flow limit until the tank empties, and needs no special electrical service.

Where 120V units and 120V outlets do fit

A 120V point-of-use heater makes sense under a bathroom or utility sink that sits a long way from the main heater, so you do not wait for hot water. It also works for a small workshop, a garage sink, or a hand-wash station. For those jobs, it does what it is made for.

There is one more place 120V shows up. The PNNL Building America Solution Center notes that gas tankless heaters still need a 120V outlet for the controls, sensors and electric spark ignition. It also says gas units produce higher flow rates than electric on-demand heaters, which is why gas is the usual answer when a household wants a shower-grade tankless.

Park Ridge note: single-family homes, permits, and electrical limits

The City of Park Ridge community profile lists 14,384 housing units, with 77 percent single-family detached and 82.8 percent owner occupied. That means most readers here own the house and make the equipment choice themselves, and many have a basement or utility room where a different heater type is easy to plan.

The city FAQ says Article 15 of the Municipal Code requires a permit for all plumbing installations or alterations. Electrical work for a 240V unit is its own trade with its own rules, so ask the Park Ridge Building Department which permits apply before any wire is run. Permit rules vary by job.

How we would help you choose

If a shower is the goal, start with the fixtures and the temperature rise, not the heater's brand. Good tankless water heater planning covers flow, gas or electric service, and venting, so the unit you buy can actually do the job.
