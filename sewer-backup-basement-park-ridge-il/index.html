Sewer Backup in a Park Ridge, IL Basement: First Steps

If sewage is coming up in your Park Ridge basement, stop using water, keep people and pets out of the wet area, and keep away from anything electrical standing in it. Then call a plumber, because the cause may be a clog in your own lateral or a full public sewer, and you need to know which.

Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage carries bacteria, so treat every wet surface as contaminated. The goal in the first minutes is to stop adding water, protect people and get help on the way.

Water that is still rising is a reason to leave the basement entirely. Once you are safe, tell the plumber what you see so they arrive with the right equipment.

  1. Stop all water use: no flushing, laundry, dishwasher or showers
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone with health risks out of the area
  3. Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or the furnace are wet, and call an electrician or the utility if unsure
  4. Open a window for air if it is safe, and do not mix cleaners
  5. Photograph the water line and damaged items before moving anything
  6. Call our 24/7 line and describe which drains are backing up

Who owns what in Park Ridge

The city FAQ on parkridge.us quotes Municipal Code 11-1-6: the homeowner repairs the sewer lateral, which is the private house service, up to and including the connection to the sewer main. That means a blockage or break between your foundation and the main is typically your repair.

The same FAQ recommends a sewer cleanout for inspection access and says tree roots do not invade structurally sound sewer pipes. If roots are in your line, that points to a crack or open joint, and a camera shows where.

The cleanout and the cleaning

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives a plumber direct access to the lateral. With one, a cable or hydro jet can clear the line, and a camera can follow it to the main. Without one, the plumber may have to pull a toilet or go through a floor drain, which takes longer and is harder on the fixtures.

After the line is open, ask for a camera pass. A clog that clears once and returns is often a sign of a bellied or cracked pipe, and our sewer line repair crew handles that work.

Overhead sewer or backwater valve

Backwater valve

  • A flap installed in the sewer line that closes when sewage flows backward.
  • Needs a floor cut and digging, and it must be kept clean and tested.
  • Protects fixtures below the valve only if the valve works.
  • Less costly than lifting the whole system in many homes.

Overhead sewer

  • Basement drains are routed to a sump pit and pumped up to the sewer line above the floor.
  • Gravity then keeps street surcharge from reaching the basement.
  • Depends on a pump and power, so a backup matters.
  • Bigger job, often chosen when finishing a basement or after repeated flooding.

Ask about permits before you protect the basement

Either system touches the sewer connection, so permits matter. The city FAQ says Article 15 of the Municipal Code requires a permit for all plumbing installations or alterations and a certificate of inspection before a new system is used. Permit rules vary by job, so we confirm with the Park Ridge Building Department.

Park Ridge note: why backups here deserve a plan

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan annex for Park Ridge says nearly 500 basements flooded on July 23, 2011. It adds that April 2013 flooding was federally declared (FEMA DR-4116), and that a flash flood on August 7, 2018 caused $100,000 in reported property damage. It describes the city's sewer system as an antiquated combination sewer and lists 17 FEMA repetitive-loss properties.

A combination sewer carries sewage and stormwater in one pipe, so a hard rain can overload it. The plan also lists ongoing sewer relining and sewer replacement in the Northwest Park and Country Club areas. For you, that means sewer backup protection is a reasonable question to ask even if your house has never flooded.

What shapes the cost of fixing and protecting

Most homeowner insurance does not pay for sewer or water backup unless you have added a sewer-backup endorsement, so check your policy before you need it. Ask your agent about coverage limits and whether failed sump pumps are included.
