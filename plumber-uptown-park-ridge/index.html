Plumbing in a district where buildings share walls and drains

Uptown is the part of Park Ridge where the street is built around shops, offices and nearby homes. A design plan prepared for the city describes a main street feel with ground floor retail and upper story residential uses. That mix matters for plumbing, because a drain or supply line in one space can affect the tenant next door or the unit above.

In a standalone house, a clog stays your problem. In a mixed use block, it may belong to the building, the landlord or an association. We start every Uptown call by asking what kind of space it is, who owns the pipe, and whether other units are wet or backing up. That short conversation saves time on site.

The city's community profile lists Uptown among its main shopping areas and names Uptown as one of two Metra commuter stations. Public restrooms and any commercial kitchens take more daily use than a typical home, which wears fixtures and drains.

Food service, restrooms and shared lines

Any business that serves food depends on floor drains and kitchen lines that carry grease and food solids. A hydro jetting visit clears the pipe wall rather than just opening a hole through the clog. Read more on our hydro jetting page.

Offices and retail spaces tend to have restrooms with several fixtures on one branch. A running toilet or a dripping faucet adds up over a month, so small repairs are worth scheduling. Our toilet repair and faucet repair pages explain what we check.

Water heaters in small commercial spaces vary a great deal. Some are tank units in a back room and some are tankless units on a wall. We confirm the type before we quote a repair or a swap. Larger or older equipment may need a permit. Park Ridge's municipal code requires permits for plumbing installations and alterations, so we confirm the rules with the Building Department.

Uptown facts and flood background for Park Ridge

These items come from published sources, and we have not added anything about specific Uptown buildings. Park Ridge's 2020 Census population was 39,656. The city's community profile lists 14,384 housing units, 82.8 percent owner occupied, with about 77 percent single family detached.

The Cook County hazard mitigation annex for Park Ridge says Uptown was developed with retail and residential projects in the 1990s and cites an Uptown Plan covering 2002 to 2025. The same annex describes an antiquated combination sewer system, meaning one pipe carries sewage and rain runoff. It records that nearly 500 basements and many streets flooded on July 23, 2011. That is a citywide record and does not single out Uptown.

Pickwick Theatre, the Art Deco landmark in Uptown Park Ridge, dates from 1928 and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1975. It is a good landmark to give when you describe where you are.

For licensing, the Illinois Department of Public Health says plumbers, apprentices and plumbing contractors are licensed under the Illinois Plumbing License Law and publishes an online lookup you can use to check any plumber.

Urgent: act now

Before we arrive: water or sewage in a shop or condo

Protect people first, then property. If the water is dirty or smells like sewage, keep customers and neighbors out of the area and tell the building manager.

  1. Close the shutoff valve for the fixture or unit if it is easy to reach.
  2. Do not run more water down a backed up drain.
  3. Move stock, electronics and paper away from the wet area.
  4. Photograph the damage before cleanup for your records.
  5. Call dispatch and say whether it is a shop, a condo or a house.
