Separating surface water problems from true plumbing problems.

Where surface water and plumbing problems overlap

Northeast Park Area is a name from the City of Park Ridge's own sewer study. The Park District lists Northeast Park at 801 N Washington, and we use it as an anchor because no boundary is published.

The study's findings for this area are specific. It reports overland flooding, ponding deeper than 6 inches, reverse slope driveways that pitch toward the house, and rear yard flooding. It sketches relief sewers and underground storage of 6 to 10 acre feet, with estimates ranging from 5.7 million to 8.8 million dollars depending on the level of protection. These are 2017 concepts, and the city can tell you what has been decided since.

For a homeowner, the useful point is that water can reach a basement from three directions. It can come from the sewer, from the ground, or from the yard. We help sort out which, because the fix is different for each.

Sewer water or surface water

Sewer backup signs

  • Water rises from the floor drain or toilet.
  • Water is dirty or smells foul.
  • It appears during heavy rain across the area.
  • Several fixtures react at once.

Surface or ground water signs

  • Clear water along a wall or at a window well.
  • Wet spot below a low door or driveway.
  • Yard ponding near the house.
  • Sump pit fills quickly and the pump runs.
What helps

Plumbing side steps that reduce risk

Working sump pump

Test it, check the discharge and the check valve. See sump pump repair.

Clear discharge

Make sure the discharge pipe sends water away from the house and does not freeze shut.

Waterproofing review

Wall seepage and window wells may need more than a pump. See basement waterproofing.

Pipe check

If water appears with no rain, look for a supply leak. See leak detection and burst pipe repair.

What is verified and what is not

Verified: the 2017 city study names the Northeast Park Area and lists the flooding findings above. The Park District lists Northeast Park at 801 N Washington. The hazard annex describes citywide combined sewers and records nearly 500 flooded basements on July 23, 2011.

Not verified: any street by street flood history, the age or material of private pipes, or the current project status. We do not claim that homes here are more likely to flood than others, only that the city documented overland flooding in this study area.

Urgent: act now

Before we arrive: water in the basement during a storm

Safety first. Do not wade into water near outlets or the electrical panel.

  1. Stay out of standing water if power may be on.
  2. Unplug items you can reach safely from dry ground.
  3. Move valuables up and away from the water.
  4. Note where the water first appeared.
  5. Call dispatch and say if it is clear, brown or smells like sewage.
