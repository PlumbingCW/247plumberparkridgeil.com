By age

What tends to wear out as a Niles building ages

Service lines

The pipe from the street main to your meter can corrode or crack, and the first signs are low pressure or a soggy patch near the curb.

Drain stacks

Cast iron rusts from the inside and narrows over decades, which is why older buildings see slow drains on several floors at once.

Galvanized supply

Steel pipe plated with zinc rusts inside, leaving rusty water and weak flow. Past a point, repiping is cheaper than chasing leaks.

Basement drains

Floor drains and old sewer laterals take the first hit when the public sewer fills, and roots find joints in aging clay.

Lead service lines and what the Village is doing about them

The Village of Niles is replacing lead service lines under a state-backed program and says it plans about 500 replacements at eligible properties between summer 2026 and summer 2027. It also says it replaces a full lead line at no cost when one is disturbed during a leak repair.

That changes how you handle a leak. If you suspect a leaking service line, call Village Public Works before anyone digs, and ask whether your line is listed as lead. The right water service line repair plan depends on which pipe is in the ground.

Floods, backflow protection and the Niles sewer

The Village's own hazard plan says more than 240 homes had flooding on July 23, 2011, with 5 to 6 feet of water in some basements. It says the Village helped 482 property owners install sewer backflow protection.

If your block was one of the wet ones, a sewer camera inspection shows whether your lateral is sound before you spend money on a valve. In 2023, the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District finished Golf Mill Stormwater Park at 9101 Greenwood Avenue, which it says gives flood relief to 100 structures across 200 acres.

Urgent: act now

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

In a house, close the main valve where the water line enters, usually near the meter in the basement. In a condo or apartment, close the unit's shutoff if it has one and tell the building manager right away, because water you stop may still be running in the floor above.

Keep people out of rooms where water is near outlets or light fixtures.

  1. Close the main valve or your unit shutoff.
  2. Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  3. Stop using sinks, tubs and toilets if a drain is backing up.
  4. Tell neighbors below you if water is coming through a ceiling.
  5. Set out buckets and lift small valuables off the floor.
  6. Call our 24/7 line and describe what you see and what you shut off.

Niles note: water source, permits and who to call

Niles is a village in Cook County, incorporated in 1899, with a 2020 Census count of 30,912. Village Hall sits on Civic Center Drive and Public Works on West Touhy Avenue. The Village's water quality report says it receives Lake Michigan water through the Morton Grove-Niles Water Commission, which buys from the City of Evanston.

Niles runs permits through its Community Development Department and an online portal, and its permit page lists guides for plumbing, water heaters and sewer repair. In Park Ridge the city owns the water line up to the buffalo box, but that is a Park Ridge rule, so in Niles ask Public Works who owns what.
