Tankless Water Heater Expansion Tank in Park Ridge, IL

A tankless water heater stores almost no hot water, so many people assume it never needs an expansion tank. The better question is whether your house plumbing is a closed system. Water expands when heated, and if a backflow preventer or check valve keeps that extra volume from pushing back into the street main, pressure rises inside the house.

In that case a thermal expansion tank may be required, and the heater manufacturer's instructions and your local inspector make the final call. In Park Ridge, IL, a dripping relief valve every time the heater fires is the usual sign. Read on for how it works and what to check.

Why heated water causes a pressure problem

Water takes up more space when it is hot. In an open system, the extra volume simply flows backward into the supply main, so pressure barely changes. In a closed system, a backflow preventer or check valve blocks that backward flow and the expanded water has nowhere to go.

The State Water Heaters installation manual says it plainly: when water is heated it expands, and provision must be made for that in a closed hot water system. It adds that a precharged diaphragm tank stores the expanded water and returns it when the temperature drops. That manual is written for tank heaters, but the physics is the same for a tankless unit on a closed house.

The Rinnai tankless manual says the same thing in its own terms. It requires means to control thermal expansion when the heater sits in a closed water supply, such as one with a backflow preventer in the cold supply line. It tells the installer to contact the water supplier or local plumbing inspector on how to do that.

You cannot tell by looking at the heater. These clues help you decide what to ask.

What the expansion tank actually does

A thermal expansion tank is a small steel tank split by a rubber diaphragm. One side holds air, the other connects to your cold water supply near the heater. When pressure rises from expanding water, the diaphragm moves and the tank absorbs the extra volume. When the water cools, the air pushes it back out.

The right size depends on the volume of water in the system, the supply pressure, and the highest temperature the water reaches. Tank makers publish sizing charts, and the air charge in the tank typically needs to match the incoming water pressure. Treat both as manufacturer instructions, not guesses, and have a gauge check your actual house pressure before choosing.

Signs your system may need expansion control

  • ✓The pressure relief valve drips or spits only while the heater is firing.
  • ✓A pressure gauge on a hose bib shows a jump when hot water heats.
  • ✓Faucets or the toilet fill valve start to leak after the heater was installed.
  • ✓Pipes bang or hiss when the heater turns on.
  • ✓You or the previous owner added a backflow preventer, check valve or new meter setup.
  • ✓The installation manual says an expansion device is required for closed systems.
  • ✓Relief valve drips stop after you open a hot tap, then return later.

The Rinnai manual also warns not to plug the pressure relief valve, and notes that periodic discharge may be due to thermal expansion. A leaking relief valve is a symptom to fix at its cause, never a valve to cap.

Park Ridge note: ask the right office, and check permits

Whether your own house is a closed system depends on what is installed on your service line and meter, not on where the water comes from. Because the Rinnai manual points you to the water supplier or local plumbing inspector, that means the Park Ridge Public Works Service Center or Building Department.

The city FAQ says Article 15 of the Municipal Code requires a permit for all plumbing installations or alterations. An expansion tank changes the plumbing, so permit rules apply, and they vary by job. We confirm them with the Park Ridge Building Department before work.

Where this fits in a heater installation

If you are adding or changing a heater, the expansion decision belongs in the plan from the start. A good tankless water heater job includes checking house pressure, asking about backflow devices, and following the manufacturer's installation manual line by line.

The same check applies to a tank replacement, which is covered under water heater installation. Homeowners in Des Plaines and Glenview often ask the same question, and we dispatch to those towns as well as Park Ridge.
