Emergency plumber

Emergency plumber in Park Ridge: what counts as an emergency

An emergency plumber visit is for water or sewage you cannot control. That includes a burst or split pipe and a main sewer line backing up into floor drains. It also includes a water heater leaking onto the floor, a sump pump that quit during heavy rain, and a toilet that keeps overflowing after you close the supply valve.

Not every call is an emergency, and we say so honestly. A dripping faucet or a slow drain can usually wait for a same-day or next-day appointment, which costs you less stress and gives the plumber room to do the job well. If you are unsure, call the 24/7 line and describe what you see. The dispatcher will tell you whether it needs to be handled now.

Park Ridge sits about 14 miles northwest of downtown Chicago and about 2 miles east of O'Hare, so the same storms that hit the city reach your street. If your basement floor drain is bubbling or your sump pit is rising, treat it as time sensitive and read the sewer line cleaning and sump pump repair pages for what to do first.

Urgent: act now

Water is on the move: do these four things first

These steps limit damage while you wait, and they apply to most plumbing emergencies in a Park Ridge house.

  1. Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement. If the valve will not turn, say so on the phone.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker, and stay out of standing water near outlets or appliances.
  3. Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets and the washer if sewage is backing up, because every flush sends more water into the same line.
  4. Move valuables off the floor, photograph the damage for your insurance claim, then call our 24/7 line.

Park Ridge plumbing: what the public record tells you

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan for Park Ridge records that nearly 500 basements flooded on July 23, 2011, that April 2013 flooding was federally declared (FEMA DR-4116), and that an August 7, 2018 flash flood caused reported property damage. The plan also blames much of the city's urban flooding on its combination sewer system, which carries storm water and household sewage in the same pipes.

What that means for you is practical. Heavy rain can push sewage and storm water back toward basement drains, so a working sump pump, a clear cleanout and an annual look at your floor drains matter more here than in a town with separate storm sewers. Our basement waterproofing page explains the options and where a plumber's work ends and a waterproofing contractor's begins.

Park Ridge also buys all of its drinking water from the City of Chicago, which draws from Lake Michigan, according to the city's water quality report. The city publishes the report each year, and it is the right place to check results before you buy a filter. Our water softener and filtration page covers how to test your own tap first.
