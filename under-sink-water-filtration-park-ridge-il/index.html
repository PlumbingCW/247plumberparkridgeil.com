Under Sink Water Filtration for Park Ridge, IL Homes

An under sink water filter for a Park Ridge, IL home is a small system at the kitchen cold tap that reduces certain things in your drinking water. A carbon filter handles chlorine taste and odor, and a reverse osmosis system removes much more. Which one fits depends on what you want removed, not on brand claims.

Park Ridge buys its water from Chicago, which draws from Lake Michigan. The city's own reports are the best starting point for deciding what to filter. Check the NSF listing for any filter you buy, because certification is specific to each contaminant.

Start with what you want out of the water

People buy filters for three reasons: taste and smell, worry about lead, or a general wish for cleaner water. These are different goals, and each calls for a different filter. A pitcher with a carbon cartridge solves the first. The second needs a filter certified for lead reduction, which you check by name on the certification listing.

Be careful with marketing claims like pure or purified. The only claim worth acting on is a certification to a named NSF/ANSI standard for the specific substance you care about.

Types of under sink systems compared

The NSF/ANSI standards are published at nsf.org. Always confirm that the exact model you are buying appears in the certified products search.

TypeWhat it is forTrade-offs
Carbon block or granular carbonChlorine taste and odor. Certified to NSF/ANSI 42 for aesthetic impuritiesSimple and compact. Does not remove everything, and cartridges need regular changes

Reading an NSF label and checking it yourself

NSF/ANSI 42 covers filters that reduce aesthetic impurities such as chlorine, taste, and odor. NSF/ANSI 53 covers filters certified to reduce contaminants with health effects, and NSF/ANSI 58 covers reverse osmosis systems. NSF also notes that certification to one standard does not mean a product reduces every contaminant.

So read the performance data sheet. Search the model on the Certified Products and Systems page at nsf.org, look for the exact contaminant you care about, and ignore a box that only shows a logo. If you cannot find the model on that list, treat the claim as unverified.

Carbon filter or reverse osmosis: a fair comparison

Carbon filter

  • Smaller footprint under the sink.
  • No drain line needed in most designs.
  • Good for chlorine taste and odor.
  • Check the listing before assuming it addresses lead.

Reverse osmosis

  • Certified under NSF/ANSI 58 for the contaminants listed.
  • Needs a drain connection and an extra faucet or hole in the sink.
  • Slower to fill a glass, because it uses a storage tank.
  • Membrane and filters need scheduled replacement.

Park Ridge note: what the city says about lead and your service line

Park Ridge's 2020 water quality report lists lead and copper results at 30 sample sites, none above the action level, and it advises flushing the tap after water has sat for several hours. Ask Public Works for the current annual report, because the 2020 numbers are not the latest.

The City's lead service page says the City owns the service line from the water main up to and including the shutoff box (called a B-box), and the property owner owns it from the B-box to the water meter. Illinois requires an inventory by 2027, the city has started it, and lead service lines must be replaced by 2044. The page links to a public dashboard and says lead lines are more likely in homes built before 1986.

For your decision: if your house is older, check the dashboard and ask the city what it knows about your line before you pick a filter. A certified filter helps at one tap, but it does not replace knowing what your service line is made of.

Before you buy or install

  • ✓Decide what you want removed: taste, a named contaminant, or both.
  • ✓Read the current annual water quality report from Park Ridge.
  • ✓Check the exact model at nsf.org under the standards that match your goal.
  • ✓Measure the cabinet space and confirm there is room for the tank and cartridges.
  • ✓Note whether your sink has a spare hole for a separate drinking faucet.
  • ✓Plan the cartridge change dates and put them on a calendar.
  • ✓Flush any new filter as the instructions say before drinking from it.

What a plumber adds to the install

Many carbon systems are a weekend job: shut off the cold supply, tee into the line, mount the housing, and check for drips. RO takes more work because it needs a drain saddle on the sink drain, a hole drilled in the sink or counter for the faucet, and tubing run cleanly so it does not kink.
