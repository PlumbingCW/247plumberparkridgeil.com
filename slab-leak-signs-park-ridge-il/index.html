Plumbing Leak in the Slab: Park Ridge, IL Guide

A plumbing leak in the slab is a leak in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete floor. It only matters if your home, or part of it, sits on a slab. If you have a full basement or crawl space under the whole house, the pipes are mostly exposed and this is probably not your problem.

The signs are a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, a water meter that keeps moving, and a musty smell or damp flooring. If you have two or more, shut off the main valve and call a plumber.

First, does this apply to your house?

Slab leaks are a real issue for slab-on-grade homes, additions built on a slab, and garages or sunrooms poured on one. They are not a concern for rooms over a basement, where pipes run in the ceiling or on the walls. The City of Park Ridge's community profile says 77 percent of its housing is single-family detached, but it does not say what foundations those homes have.

So look at your own house. If you are not sure which rooms sit on concrete, check where the main water line enters, and note which floors have no space beneath them.

The signs, in the order people usually notice them

A warm patch on the floor is the classic sign, because a leaking hot line heats the concrete above it. You may feel it with bare feet or see it as a dry spot after you mop. Pets will often lie on it.

Next comes sound. With every tap and appliance off, a hiss or trickle from under the floor suggests a pressurized line is leaking. You may also see higher water bills, low pressure, or a water heater that seems to run constantly. A musty smell, damp carpet, bubbling flooring, or cracks in the slab or walls are signs of moisture rising from below.

Check the meter before you call

1

Stop all use

Turn off every faucet, appliance, ice maker, and irrigation zone, and make sure nobody flushes.

2

Read the meter

Look at the leak indicator or low-flow dial. If it keeps turning, water is leaving somewhere.

3

Close the main

Shut the house main valve. If the meter stops moving, the leak is inside the house.

4

Call a plumber

Tell us what you saw. A locating visit uses listening gear and cameras to find the leak.

How a slab leak is found

A plumber starts by pressure testing the lines to confirm a leak and to find out which line it is on. Electronic listening equipment, such as a ground microphone, then picks up the sound of escaping water, and a thermal camera can show a warm hot-water line under the floor. Together they allow a precise spot to be marked before any concrete is opened.

That matters because breaking up a slab in the wrong place is expensive and does not stop the leak. Our leak detection work focuses on locating before cutting.

Repair options and their trade-offs

Open the slab and fix the pipe

  • Direct repair at the exact spot.
  • Involves breaking and patching concrete and flooring.
  • Makes sense for a single, clearly located leak.
  • Messy and disruptive indoors.

Reroute or repipe above the slab

  • Abandons the leaking line and runs new pipe through walls or the attic.
  • Less concrete work and avoids repeat leaks in the same line.
  • Better when pipe is old or leaks have recurred.
  • Higher up-front scope, which a plumber can price clearly.

Park Ridge note: the meter and your bill

The City of Park Ridge's resident FAQ says the City maintains water meters and bills every other month. It also says the city's WaterSmart portal offers free leak alerts. Because bills arrive every two months, a hidden leak can run for weeks before the number looks wrong.

For you, the lesson is to check the meter yourself instead of waiting for a bill, and to sign up for WaterSmart alerts. If the leak is on the pipe between the shutoff box and your house, the city's FAQ puts that water service pipe on the homeowner, which affects who pays for the repair.

What to do right away if you suspect a slab leak

  • ✓Run the meter check described above.
  • ✓Shut off the main valve if you hear water or see damp flooring.
  • ✓Turn off the water heater if the hot line may be involved.
  • ✓Move rugs, furniture, and valuables off wet or warm flooring.
  • ✓Take dated photos of damage for your insurance file.
  • ✓Read your homeowner policy to see what water damage it covers.
  • ✓Call a licensed plumber and ask for a clear price before work starts.
