Boiler Expansion Tank Repair in Park Ridge, IL

An expansion tank on a hydronic boiler gives heated water somewhere to go. Water grows in volume as it warms, and a closed heating loop has no room for that growth, so a sealed tank with an air cushion absorbs it and keeps pressure steady.

When the tank fails, pressure climbs every heating cycle, the relief valve may drip, and the system can run noisy or lose heat. If you see that, call for boiler repair and do not keep topping off the boiler.

What the tank does inside the heating loop

In a closed hot water heating system, the boiler heats water that circulates through baseboards, radiators or in-floor tubing. When that water goes from cold to hot, it expands. The tank holds an air charge behind a rubber diaphragm or bladder, and the extra water compresses the air.

As the system cools, the air pushes water back out and pressure falls to normal. This cycle repeats every time the boiler fires, which is why a tank with no air cushion causes trouble fast.

Pressure behavior to watch

A healthy system sits at a stable cold pressure and rises modestly when hot. Check the gauge when the boiler is cold, then again at full temperature. The cold fill value is often marked on the boiler or in its manual, so read it before judging.

If pressure jumps well above that figure, or the needle swings up on each cycle, the tank is the first suspect. If the gauge sits near zero, the system has lost water, which is a leak or auto-fill issue and a different problem.

Relief valve discharge: do not ignore it

The relief valve is the safety device that opens when pressure gets too high. Residential boiler valves are often rated near 30 psi, but check the label on yours. A dripping valve means pressure is reaching that limit, so find the cause instead of replacing the valve alone or capping the line. Hot discharge can scald, so keep clear of the outlet pipe.

Repair, replace or recharge

Recharge or adjust

  • Works only when the tank is a healthy diaphragm type and has lost some air.
  • Done with the system cold and isolated, using a tire gauge on the air valve.
  • Cheap in parts, but it only helps if the diaphragm is intact.
  • Water coming out of the air valve means the diaphragm is gone.

Replace the tank

  • Right choice when the tank is waterlogged, rusted or leaking.
  • Needs isolating and draining part of the loop, then purging air.
  • Sized for the system volume and set to the cold fill pressure.
  • A good time to check the relief valve, the feeder and the air separator.

How a service visit goes

1

Read pressure

2

3

4

The new tank is charged to match fill pressure and air is bled from the system.

Plumber or heating contractor

Hydronic boilers sit between plumbing and heating. Piping, valves, tanks, feeders and relief lines are plumbing work, while burners, controls and gas train are heating work. Some companies handle both, and others split them.

Ask whoever you call whether they service hydronic boilers and both the water side and the burner. A tank swap and pressure adjustment is often within a plumber's scope, while a combustion or control fault may need a heating specialist. Our licensed plumbers can look at the water side and tell you where the problem sits.

Park Ridge note: winters and permits

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan annex for Park Ridge notes that winter storms can bring more than 10 inches of snow, and Wikipedia lists the area in USDA Plant Hardiness Zone 5b. For you, that means a heating failure on a cold night is more than an inconvenience, so a tank issue is worth fixing before January.

The city FAQ on parkridge.us says Article 15 of the Municipal Code requires a permit for plumbing installations or alterations. Permit rules vary by job, so we confirm with the Park Ridge Building Department before a larger boiler replacement. A simple tank swap may be treated differently from a full boiler replacement, and new piping work falls under plumbing installation, which is why we ask.

Nearby towns

We hear the same tank and pressure symptoms from homes in Niles and Norridge, where older hydronic systems are common. The advice is the same anywhere. Do not keep adding water to chase pressure, and have the tank checked.

Code and licensing for boiler work

Boiler work mixes plumbing and heating trades. Plumbing connections follow the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, and according to the Illinois Department of Public Health, plumbers are licensed under the Illinois Plumbing License Law. Ask who is licensed for each part before you book.
