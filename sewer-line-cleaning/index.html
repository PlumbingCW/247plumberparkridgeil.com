When sewer lines back up: storms, thaws and slow builds

Sewer backups tend to arrive in three ways. The first is the slow build: a drain that gurgles for weeks, then fails on the day you run laundry and a dishwasher together. The second is a storm, when heavy rain pushes water into the system faster than it can leave.

The third is the thaw. After a hard winter, shifted soil and loosened roots can leave a pipe misaligned or partly blocked, and the problem shows up in spring. Each pattern points to a different fix, which is why the first thing we ask is when the backup started and what the weather was doing.

Urgent: act now

Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage carries bacteria. The goal while you wait is to stop adding water, protect people and limit what the water touches.

  1. Stop using water everywhere: no flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher.
  2. Keep people and pets out of the wet area. Do not wade in sewage barefoot.
  3. If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out of that room and do not touch anything electrical.
  4. Move valuables, boxes and rugs off the floor only if you can do it without touching the water.
  5. Open a window if there is a sewer smell, and wash hands well after any contact.
  6. Note whether neighbors have the same problem, and tell us. That suggests the public main.

City main or your lateral: who owns the blockage

Your house drain leaves the building and joins the public sewer in the street. The pipe on your side of that connection is the sewer lateral. The Park Ridge city FAQ quotes Municipal Code 11-1-6, which makes the homeowner responsible for repairing the lateral up to and including the connection to the sewer main.

In practice that means a clog or crack between your house and the main is yours to clear and fix. A blockage in the main itself is the city's, and Public Works handles it. If neighbors are backed up too, it is worth a call to the city while we clear your side.

Before it fails

Signs of a mainline blockage

A main sewer blockage often announces itself before the backup. These signs usually show on the lowest fixtures first.

Gurgling toilets

Air pushed back through the trap when water drains somewhere else in the house.

Floor drain bubbles

The basement floor drain sits lowest and shows trouble first.

Multiple slow drains

When showers, sinks and the washer all slow together, the shared line is the suspect.

Sewage smell indoors

A stronger smell near the drain or in the basement points to a partial blockage or a dry trap.

Storm drain cleaning: yard drains, window wells and downspout lines

Not every drain that overflows in a storm is a sewer line. Yard drains, driveway drains, window well drains and buried downspout lines carry rain, and they clog with leaves and silt. We clear those with a cable or a jetter and a camera when needed, and only on the property side.

Street inlets and the city's storm lines belong to Park Ridge. If you are not sure who owns a drain, ask the Public Works department before paying to clear it. Where the same pipe carries both storm and sanitary flow, treat it as a sewer backup and call right away.

Prevention before the next storm

A few habits and one inspection cut the odds of a repeat.

  • ✓Find your cleanout and keep the cap on tight and the area clear. The city recommends one for inspection access.
  • ✓Do not flush wipes, even if the package says flushable. Keep grease out of kitchen drains.
  • ✓Spread out laundry loads during heavy rain so the line is not full when the street sewer is.
  • ✓Ask about a backwater valve, which blocks reverse flow. Permit rules vary by job, so we confirm with the Park Ridge Building Department.
  • ✓Book a sewer camera inspection after any serious backup so you know the condition of the line.
  • ✓Check your insurance policy for water backup coverage before the wet season.

Park Ridge note: a combination sewer with a flood record

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan annex for Park Ridge names the city's antiquated combination sewer system as a driver of urban flooding. It records nearly 500 basements flooded on July 23, 2011, federally declared flooding in April 2013 (FEMA DR-4116), and a flash flood on August 7, 2018 with $100,000 in reported property damage. It also lists sewer relining and sewer replacement projects as mitigation work.

Because storm water and household waste share pipes in a combination system, heavy rain can push wastewater back through a home's lateral. Cleaning a clear lateral will not stop that, so a backwater valve is a separate decision. Ask us during the visit, and read about sump pump repair if your basement also relies on a pump.
