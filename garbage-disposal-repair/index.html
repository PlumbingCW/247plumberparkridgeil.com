Emergency garbage disposal repair in Park Ridge, IL is for a disposal that is leaking under the sink, jammed, backed up or tripping power, and our licensed plumbers answer the 24/7 line for it. A garbage disposal is a grinder mounted under the sink drain that chops food scraps small enough to wash through the pipes.

Before anything else, switch it off at the wall and keep your hand out of it. A leaking or backed-up unit can be made safe for the night and fixed on a same-day appointment when available.

Disposal leaking or backed up right now: what to do first

A disposal combines a motor, household power and standing water, so safety comes before cleanup. Switch it off at the wall switch, and turn the breaker off too if there is water near the outlet or cord. Our 24/7 line is open at night and on weekends if the leak will not wait.

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall and do not run it again
  2. Turn off the breaker if water is near the outlet, cord or wiring
  3. Stop using the sink and the dishwasher, since both drain through the disposal
  4. Set a bucket under the leak and wipe the cabinet floor dry
  5. Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up sink
  6. Photograph the leak and any damage before you clean up

Jams, humming and the reset button

A humming disposal with still blades is jammed. Switch it off at the wall and unplug it or switch off the breaker. Then look at the bottom of the unit for a hex-shaped socket. Work a hex key back and forth in it until the blades turn freely, and pull out whatever caused the jam with tongs, never your fingers.

Wait a few minutes, then press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit. If it clicks and the disposal runs, the overload protector tripped during the jam. If it trips again right away, stop and call, because repeated resets can burn the motor.

If nothing happens at all, check the wall switch, the outlet under the sink if the unit plugs in, and the breaker. Hard-wired units have no outlet to check. Never reach in or poke around while the unit is still connected to power.

Leak points

Where the water is coming from

Where a disposal leaks tells you more than any other clue. Dry everything with a towel, run water for a minute, and watch for the first drop.

Top of the unit

Water where the disposal meets the sink flange means the mounting or the putty seal has failed. It is often a reseal and not a new unit.

Side of the unit

The dishwasher inlet and the drain outlet both attach at the side. A loose clamp or a worn gasket is the usual cause.

Bottom or body

A leak through the housing, or a puddle from the underside, means the inner seal has failed. Replacement is the answer.

The pipe after the unit

The trap and drain arm sit just past the disposal, and a loose slip nut there can look like a disposal leak. Tightening or replacing the trap solves it.

Humming, grinding and kitchen odors

A disposal that grinds slowly or seems weak is often fighting a partial clog downstream, not a failing motor. If the sink also drains slowly with the disposal off, the blockage is in the pipe past the unit, and cabling it is a job for drain cleaning.

Odors usually come from a film of food on the underside of the rubber splash guard, and from scraps clinging to the grinding chamber. Scrub the guard with a brush and dish soap, then run cold water while the disposal runs. If the smell survives a good cleaning, it may be coming from the drain line or trap, not the unit.

Dishwasher drain connection and the air gap

The dishwasher drain hose usually connects to a small inlet on the side of the disposal. A new disposal has a plug inside that inlet called a knockout, and it must be removed when a dishwasher is connected. If the knockout stays in place, dishwasher water has nowhere to go and backs up into the dishwasher.

A dishwasher also needs protection so that dirty sink water cannot flow back into it. That protection is either a high loop in the drain hose or an air gap, a small fitting on the counter or the sink deck. If dishwasher water bubbles up in the sink, the hose, the air gap or the disposal inlet is blocked.

We confirm which arrangement fits your sink and what the Illinois Plumbing Code requires for your setup. We can also reattach a dishwasher line during a disposal visit.

Choosing and installing a replacement disposal

Horsepower is the main spec. Smaller units around one third horsepower suit light use, while half horsepower and above grind more finely and jam less often, which matters for a household that cooks most nights. Higher horsepower also costs more and is usually larger under the sink.

The mount is the next choice. Quick-lock mounts hang the unit on a twist-on ring and install fast, while bolted mounts are sturdier but take more time. Continuous-feed units run while the switch is on, and batch-feed units run only when a stopper is in place, which some households prefer for safety.

Installation covers the sink flange, the drain and dishwasher connections, and the power. A plug-in cord needs a suitable outlet, and a hard-wired unit needs a safe connection to a switch. Park Ridge's code requires a permit for plumbing installations or alterations, so we confirm what your job needs with the Park Ridge Building Department. Larger kitchen changes fall under plumbing installation.

What must never go down a disposal

Most jams and clogs start with something that did not belong in the unit.

  • ✓Fats, oils and grease, which cool and coat the pipe.
  • ✓Large bones and fruit pits that can jam or dull the blades.
  • ✓Fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.
  • ✓Starchy foods like rice, pasta and potato peels in large amounts.
  • ✓Glass, metal, plastic, bottle caps and utensils.
  • ✓Harsh chemical drain cleaners, which can damage seals and splash on you.
  • ✓Paint, solvents and other household chemicals.

Electrical safety before you touch anything

Switch the disposal off at the wall, and turn off its breaker before you reach under the sink, since a wall switch can be flipped by someone else. Never put your hand or any tool other than tongs or a hex key into the grinding chamber. If water has reached the outlet or cord, leave the breaker off and call.

A sudden leak that soaks the cabinet and flooring may be covered by a homeowner policy, but a worn disposal is usually not. Backup from a clogged line may need a separate endorsement, so check your policy and keep your photos.

Park Ridge note: kitchen grease, your private line and the city sewer

Under Municipal Code 11-1-6, as quoted on the city FAQ, the homeowner repairs the sewer lateral from the house up to and including the connection to the sewer main. Grease from a kitchen sink settles in that private pipe, and any repair there is yours. Keeping fat out of the disposal protects a pipe you own. If the pipe itself has failed, sewer line repair is the next step.

The city FAQ also recommends a sewer cleanout for inspection access. If your kitchen sink backs up with the disposal off and the trap clear, the blockage is past the disposal, and a cleanout lets a plumber cable the line. When a clog keeps returning, sewer line cleaning clears the main run.

Code notes for disposal work

A disposal ties into the sink drain and the home's electrical supply, so each half has its own rules, according to the Illinois Plumbing Code and the electrical code that applies to your home. The plumbing half follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, and the wiring half should be handled by someone qualified for electrical work.
