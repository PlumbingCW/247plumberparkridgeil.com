When Norridge basements flood

Most flooding here follows the same pattern: a thunderstorm drops several inches of rain in a short window, the sump pit fills, and the pump either keeps up or does not. The Cook County hazard plan annex for Norridge lists 4.25 inches of rain on July 22, 2011 and 4.8 inches on April 18, 2013, both with street and basement flooding. April 2013 was a federal disaster declaration.

The same annex says precipitation in the Chicago area peaks in May and June, and that heavy fall thunderstorms can also flood. A pump that works in March can still fail in June, so watch the pit during the first big storm of the season.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

If a pipe has let go, close the main valve inside the house, then open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain what is left. Do not touch the shutoff in the parkway, called a buffalo box, because the village's own permit form warns that tampering with it can lead to arrest and prosecution.

If the basement is taking on storm water instead, stay out of it once water reaches outlets or the furnace.

  1. Close the main valve inside the house.
  2. Leave the parkway buffalo box alone.
  3. Stay out of a flooded basement if water is near outlets, the panel or the furnace.
  4. Unplug the sump pump only if the cord is dry and out of the water.
  5. Move small valuables upstairs and set out buckets for ceiling drips.
  6. Call our 24/7 line and tell dispatch what you shut off.

Winter in Norridge: cold snaps and frozen lines

The annex describes temperatures that often stay below freezing for days or weeks in January and February, and a January 2014 storm that dropped 13.8 inches of snow. A pipe in an outside wall, a crawl space or an unheated garage is the first to freeze.

When a frozen pipe splits, the water often does not show until it thaws. If one tap runs weak in a hard freeze, shut the main and call, because burst pipe repair costs far less than the flooded room that follows a thaw.

Repair the pump or replace it?

Repair

  • Fits a stuck float, a clogged intake or a failed check valve.
  • Quicker and keeps the existing pit.
  • Leaves an old motor in service.

Replace

  • Fits a pump that has run constantly for years.
  • Better before storm season than during it.
  • Costs more up front than a part swap.

Where the water goes after the pump

A pump only moves water, so the discharge matters. The village's sanitary sewer permit sheet says storm water may not enter the sanitary sewer and downspouts may not connect to it, so ask the Building Department where a sump discharge may go before changing the piping.

If the pit overflows while the pump runs, the pipe may be undersized. The check valve may be stuck, or the ground may be saturated. Sump pump repair looks at each, and basement waterproofing is the longer answer when seepage keeps coming through the walls.

A spring and fall check that prevents most calls

Do these before the wet months and again before the first hard freeze.

  • ✓Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch the pump start, run and shut off.
  • ✓Check that the discharge pipe runs well away from the foundation and is not frozen or blocked.
  • ✓Confirm the check valve clicks shut when the pump stops.
  • ✓Find the main shutoff valve and turn it once so it is not stuck.
  • ✓Disconnect outdoor hoses before the first freeze.

Norridge note: a small village with older homes

Norridge is a village in Cook County, incorporated in 1948 and made a home rule community by referendum in 1973, with a 2020 Census count of 15,251 and 82.4 percent owner occupancy. The hazard annex describes it as land-locked and nearly fully developed, with older homes, many without air conditioning. Chicago lies north and south, Harwood Heights is to the east, and forest preserves run along the west, with O'Hare about a mile north.

The annex says parts of the village were natural creek areas before development, particularly along Oketo and Octavia Avenues, and lists drainage projects near the Eisenhower Public Library. The Building Department at 4000 North Olcott Avenue issues plumbing permits under the Illinois Plumbing Code and requires rough and final inspections.
